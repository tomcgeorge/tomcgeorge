Baselyst sets up everything a new product needs to run: company structure, a delivery pipeline, AI agents, security, launch marketing and legal groundwork. One framework, tailored to you, in accounts you own.

### How we build

`Idea` → `Spec and decisions` → `Agents build` → `Automated checks` → `Human approval` → `Release`<br>
<sub>Changes requested at approval go straight back to the agents.</sub>

Requirements, tasks and architecture decisions are written down before any code. Agents build under one shared rulebook. Every pull request runs AI code and security review, tests, and secret and dependency scans, and each release is built once and promoted from Dev to Prod.

Agents do most of the typing. A person approves every merge, every migration and anything that touches a secret. Where a decision has to stand up to an audit, such as a risk grade, there is no AI in the path at all.

## 📖 Background

I started as one of the first engineers at **JustDoc**, a healthcare startup, working across backend and operations while it grew from 80 to 1,200+ daily orders and was acquired by **Netmeds**. At Netmeds I scaled the platform to 5,000+ daily orders and moved it to AWS for stronger compliance, ISO 27001 aligned controls and room to grow across online pharmacy orders, lab test bookings and doctor consultations. At **Formpak** I took a legacy on premises B2B SaaS product through a full move to AWS, with cloud and AI native development: a new landing zone, a multitenant SaaS platform, SRE practices, FinOps and a team that grew from 4 to 10.

My MSc in Enterprise Systems Management (Distinction) at Birmingham City University changed how I look at all of it. I stopped seeing infrastructure as the goal and started treating it as part of the business: process improvement, digital transformation, and security and automation designed in from the first day rather than bolted on later.

Today I think about systems the way a CTO has to. Start from the outcome the business needs. Treat security and cost as design inputs. Write decisions down so the next person understands why. Measure what matters and ignore what flatters.

That path shaped how I work: **automation over heroics, runbooks over tribal knowledge, and systems that outlive the person who built them.** Not every problem needs the newest tool. Often the right answer is the boring, reliable one.
